Black hole perturbation theory · Gravitational waves

Changkai Chen陈昌凯

I develop analytical and numerical methods for black hole perturbation theory, with applications to gravitational radiation and black hole spectroscopy. My early research focused on high-order numerical algorithms for fluid dynamics. I then developed radiation calculations for perturbed black holes during my master's studies with Prof. Jiliang Jing, before extending this work to black hole spectra during my doctorate with Prof. Zhoujian Cao.

01 / RESEARCH

Methods for the strong-field universe.

01

Gravitational-wave fluxes

I derived connection formulas for confluent Heun functions, the special functions used to represent the perturbation solutions, to determine scattering amplitudes. This work extends radiation calculations from circular Schwarzschild orbits to generic Kerr motion. The latest implementation computes energy fluxes more efficiently than the comparison methods in the published benchmarks, providing orbit-averaged dissipative information for gravitational self-force modelling.

Read the 2026 paper ↗
02

Complete Spectroscopy of Kerr Black Holes

I compute Schwarzschild and Kerr quasinormal mode spectra, including branches near the negative imaginary frequency axis and the spectrum at exact Kerr extremality. I also track Kerr total-transmission mode families and exceptional points, using scattering amplitudes to distinguish modes by their radiation boundary conditions.
